# Omnichannel_Sales_Dashboard
# Omnichannel Sales Analysis — Excel Dashboard

## About the Project

I built this dashboard to get a clearer picture of how a retail business is performing across its products, sales channels and regions. The dataset contains 2,708 records and 20 columns, covering orders, customer types, product categories, pricing, discounts, revenue, costs and gross profit.

I wanted to bring these details together in one place so it would be easier to compare monthly performance, see which categories contribute the most revenue and identify the products selling the highest number of units.

## Key Findings

- **Total revenue reached $645,224, with $296,385 in gross profit.** This represents an overall gross margin of approximately 45.9%, based on the dashboard totals.

- **Camping and Hiking account for most of the revenue shown.** Camping generated $198,505, followed by Hiking at $179,959. Together, they contributed $378,464 — around 58.7% of total revenue. Fitness was the smallest category at $55,553.

- **June was the strongest month for revenue, but November also stood out.** Revenue peaked at approximately $70,000 in June, before falling to around $49,000 in October. November recovered to roughly $66,000, followed by a decline to $51,000 in December. Gross profit followed a similar pattern, reaching around $31,000 in both June and July.

- **Trail Boots and Summit Backpack 45L lead the bestseller chart.** Trail Boots recorded 449, followed closely by Summit Backpack 45L at 447. Compact Sleeping Bag followed at 433. The small gap between the top two products makes this a closer comparison than the chart might initially suggest.

- **Revenue is concentrated in a few product categories.** Camping alone contributed approximately 30.8% of total revenue, while Fitness contributed about 8.6%. This highlights the difference in revenue contribution across the categories and provides a useful starting point for further product-level analysis.

## What the Dashboard Includes

- KPI cards for total sales, gross profit, average price and units sold.
- A monthly comparison of revenue and gross profit.
- Revenue by product category.
- A top-five bestseller comparison.
- Interactive filters for sales channel, customer type, region and order date.

## What I Took Away From This Project

I wanted this project to be more than a collection of charts. Bringing revenue, gross profit, categories and individual products together helped me see how different views of the same sales data can tell different parts of the business story.

It also gave me practical experience building an Excel dashboard where someone can filter the results and explore the performance they are interested in, rather than relying on a single fixed report.
